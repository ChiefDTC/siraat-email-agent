Weak or do-not angles from the same data: comparison listicles ("We rated every titanium pan" ROAS 0.70, "Your pans rated out of 10" 0.89, "Wait till you see number 3" 0.65) get clicks but not orders, keep them for browse only. Chef Rating copy on cold traffic runs at 1.30. German-language variants sit at 0.34 to 0.54. Founder confession videos 0.81.

Notion do-not list (W40 and W41 briefs, applies to mail copy too): no 12-piece set in the Hammered Pan Pro story, no competitor named, no offer or price before the proof beat, no claims beyond zero coatings, Light Labs tested, 75-year warranty, 30-day free returns; say "titanium cooking surface", never "pure" or "solid" titanium in new copy; no health scare, no fake urgency or countdown without a real end date; no em dashes.

## C. Angle to flow-email mapping

Rule of thumb from the data: cold reads proof and chef first (angles 1, 2, 6), warm reads price and risk reversal (3, 4), everyone reads replacement cost (5) and the GIFs (9) carry the body. Offer only after the proof beat.

### Welcome (5)
1. Welcome 1, hero: angle 1. Subject "Has your cookware actually been tested?" Preview "Professional performance. Zero PFAS." Body: Light Labs report image, hammered close-up GIF, no offer above the fold, welcome code at the end.
2. Welcome 2, education: angle 2 + stainless mistake. Subject "You read the labels. Did you check your pan?" Opener "I've burned through hundreds of pans in 22 years as a chef."
3. Welcome 3, objection: angle 5 + competitor angle 2. Subject "How many pans have you thrown out since 2020?" Body: drawer of dead pans, cost-per-year math table.
4. Welcome 4, lifestyle: angle 10 + competitor angle 5. Subject "POV: you actually have time for a slow breakfast." Egg-release GIF, one recipe, soft CTA.
5. Welcome 5, offer: angle 3 + 4. Subject "ENJOY 50% OFF (and 100 days to change your mind)". Offer stack tiles exactly as live on site.

### Checkout abandonment (4)
1. Checkout 1 (1 h): angle 4. Subject "Still thinking? It's covered for 75 years." Risk reversal only, no discount.
2. Checkout 2 (24 h): angle 8 as a comment reply. Subject "Can you drop the price? I'm on a budget." Body answers with the live offer.
3. Checkout 3 (48 h): angle 7. Subject "What's the catch?" Text-thread format from W40: every objection answered by a proof point or offer element.
4. Checkout 4 (72 h): angle 3 + urgency only if a real end date exists. Subject "The sale ends." One line, one button.

### Cart abandonment (3)
1. Cart 1: angle 9. Subject "One pass with a damp cloth." Cleaning GIF, no offer.
2. Cart 2: angle 5 + competitor angle 1. Subject "The pan you keep replacing is the expensive one." Math table.
3. Cart 3: angle 4 + 3. Subject "Try it risk free for 100 days" with offer reminder.

### Browse abandonment (2)
1. Browse 1: angle 6. Subject "Obviously staged. (It wasn't.)" HexClad scratch-test comparison GIF, product link.
2. Browse 2: competitor angle 4. Subject "Week 1, Month 1, Month 3." Skeptic diary with real customer lines.

### Post-purchase (3)
1. Post-purchase 1 (order confirmation plus): angle 4 reassurance. Subject "You bought a pan from an ad. Explain yourself." (W40 hook 3, flipped to reassurance: 75-year warranty, 30-day returns, how to season nothing because there is no coating).
2. Post-purchase 2 (delivery): angle 10 + competitor angle 5. Subject "First egg. No oil." Egg-release GIF, three recipes, ask for the egg video back.
3. Post-purchase 3 (day 21): competitor angle 3. Subject "The one gift category worth getting right." Give-one framing, bundle or crepe pan cross-sell, review ask.

### Winback (2)
1. Winback 1: angle 7. Subject "We need to publicly apologize." W41 confession frame: the lie is "the last pan I'll ever buy" (it is covered for 75 years). Offer in a separate block.
2. Winback 2: angle 3 + warehouse story. Subject "Warehouse clearance: up to 50% off." Only when the warehouse offer is live; otherwise swap to the replacement-fatigue static (ROAS 2.43).
