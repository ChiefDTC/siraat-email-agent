Hi {{ first_name|default:'there' }}, your pan is either on your stove already or on its way to you. Whether it keeps its promises, the first egg will tell you. We cannot see which, so here is the chef's first cook now. Keep it for the morning you unbox it.

There is no coating, so the release comes from heat and timing, not chemicals. Cold metal grabs your food. Hot metal lets it go.

THE CHEF'S FIRST COOK
1
Preheat on medium, 2 to 3 minutes
Medium on titanium works like high on a normal pan. You never need to go higher.
2
Do the water test
Flick in a few drops. When they bead up and glide instead of sizzling away, the pan is ready.
3
Add a thin layer of oil
First heat, then oil. Always into a hot pan, never a cold one.
4
Eggs: turn the heat down to low
Give it a moment. Once the white sets, it lets go by itself, and then it slides.
5
Meat and fish: lay it down, then leave it
It holds on at first, then forms a crust and lets go on its own. Moving it early is what makes it stick.
IF SOMETHING STICKS
A sticky pan is not a ruined pan
Usually the pan was not hot enough yet, or the food moved too early. Mix baking soda with a little water, rub it over the surface with a soft sponge and rinse. Back to day one. A blue or gold shimmer is harmless heat tint.
