Hi Sarah,

Your order arrived yesterday. Before the first cook, one thing worth knowing: there is no coating on your pan. That is the whole point, and it means the release comes from heat and timing, not from chemicals.

Our chef puts it simply: if you can do an egg, you can do anything. Here is how, step by step.

THE CHEF'S FIRST COOK
1
Preheat on medium, 2 to 3 minutes
Medium on titanium works like high on a normal pan. You never need to go higher.
2
Do the water test
Flick in a few drops of water. When they bead up and skate across the pan instead of sizzling away, it is ready.
3
Add a thin layer of oil
Just enough to coat the surface. First heat, then oil. Always into a hot pan, never a cold one.
4
Eggs: turn the heat down to low
Crack the egg in and give it a moment. Once the white sets, it lets go by itself, and then it slides.
5
Meat and fish: lay it down, then leave it
It will hold on at first. As it cooks it forms a crust and releases on its own. Moving it early is what makes it stick.
IF SOMETHING STICKS
A sticky pan is not a ruined pan
Mix baking soda with a little water into a paste, rub it over the surface with a soft sponge, rinse, and the pan is back to day one. Then start again from step 1, with a little more patience on the preheat.
Looking after it
Less work than you are used to.
Dishwasher safe
Let it cool first, then the dishwasher or warm water and a soft sponge.
Rainbow tint is harmless
A blue or gold shimmer after high heat is heat tint on the metal. It does not affect cooking.
No seasoning, ever
Metal utensils are fine. There is no coating to protect.
